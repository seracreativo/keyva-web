How it works

1 · Send us your account code

Open Keyva on your Mac, go to Settings → Premium → Manage & Restore, and press Show my account code. It is 22 characters and it identifies your iCloud account without telling us who you are — we never see your name or your email address through it.

2 · We send you a payment link

By return email. Nothing is charged until you use it.

3 · You get your licence

A line of text starting with KV1. Paste it into the same panel and press Activate. It is stored in your iCloud keychain, so it appears on your other devices on its own — including the App Store copies on your iPhone and iPad.

Why the code, and not just an email address

The licence is signed with your account code inside it, so it works on every device you own and on nobody else's. That code only exists on your Mac; at the moment you pay, we do not know it yet. Asking afterwards is a round trip that gets lost, so the app writes the email for you.

Or just subscribe

The App Store has Keyva at $4.99 a month or $24.99 a year, and it covers your iPhone and iPad too. Unless you plan to stay a year and a half or more, that is the cheaper way — and it cancels itself when you stop. A licence is for people who would rather not have another subscription.

What happens after twelve months

Nothing, unless you want something newer. Keyva keeps working with Premium on every version published while your licence ran. Renewing buys the versions after that. It holds the credentials you work with every day; locking you out of them because a date passed would be the wrong way round.

Questions

Can I use it on my iPhone?

Your licence carries over through the iCloud keychain, so the App Store copies on your other devices recognise it. What they cannot do is activate one — that only happens here, on the Mac.

Is there a refund?

Write to us within 14 days and we return it, no questions. Try the free plan first: it does not expire.

I bought a subscription on the App Store. Do I need this?

No. Either one gives you Premium everywhere. Having both changes nothing.
